Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Youngstown, Youngstown Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
