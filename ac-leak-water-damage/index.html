AC Leak Water Damage Help in Youngstown

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Our Process

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Failure of the small pump that lifts condensate to a drain.
  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.
