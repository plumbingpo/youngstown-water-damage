Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Youngstown

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Contain the WaterFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Coverage

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceiling Drywall

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Rot in these members weakens the structure that holds the roof up.

Hidden Attic Mold

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.
