Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Youngstown

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Stored Belongings

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Get the Water Out

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.
