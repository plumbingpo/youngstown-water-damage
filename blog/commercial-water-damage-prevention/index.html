Protecting Your Business From Water Damage in Youngstown, OH

Home / Blog / Commercial Water Damage Prevention in Youngstown, OH

For owners and managers of commercial property in Youngstown, OH, few risks cost as much as water damage. A water loss at a business interrupts operations, ruins inventory, displaces staff, and can expose you to liability, all in addition to the restoration bill. Proactive prevention is far more cost-effective than reactive restoration. Below are the most effective ways to keep water damage away from a commercial property in Youngstown.

Start With Scheduled Building Maintenance

Water damage prevention rests on a thorough maintenance program for the building. Inspect the building envelope, fire suppression systems, HVAC equipment, plumbing, and roofing on a regular schedule. Keep written records of every repair and inspection, since they show due diligence, which can limit your liability and back up an insurance claim after a loss.

Leak Detection Technology

Commercial water detection systems provide early warning of leaks before they cause significant damage. Today's equipment is able to monitor supply lines, water heaters, places that tend to flood, and HVAC condensate lines. Some systems automatically shut off water supply when a leak is detected. Detection technology is an investment that typically earns back its price many times if it stops just one major water loss.

Have an Emergency Plan Ready

Plan ahead for a water emergency at your commercial property by listing emergency contacts for the restoration contractor (Youngstown Restoration, (979) 290-9464), property management, and your insurance agent, training staff on the main water shutoff procedure, assigning employee roles and duties for a water event, setting procedures that protect inventory and critical documents, and agreeing on how to communicate with stakeholders and tenants.

Insurance Points to Check

Your commercial property coverage deserves an annual review with your insurance agent. Confirm that it has endorsements for sewer and drain backup and equipment breakdown, enough coverage for the building and its contents, and business interruption insurance. Comprehensive coverage raises premiums by a relatively modest amount, so compare that with the cost of being underinsured.
