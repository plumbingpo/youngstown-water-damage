Kitchen Water Damage Help in Youngstown

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in Youngstown, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
